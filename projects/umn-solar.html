UMN Solar Vehicle, Power-Distribution Board

Altium · TPS54560 · power

A power-distribution board I designed in Altium for the University of Minnesota Solar Vehicle Project. I laid it out, reflow-soldered it, and it's running on this year's car.

What it needed to do

The car's driver interface runs on a Raspberry Pi, and the team moved it to a Pi HAT that needs 12 V and more current than the usual 5 V setup. So I designed a separate board to handle that power cleanly and keep it apart from the rest of the electronics. A large connector links it to the battery and to the UI control board, which sends the enable signals.

Load switch and buck

A high-side P-channel MOSFET works as a load switch, so the UI control board can turn the 12 V to the Pi HAT on and off with a logic-level enable signal. A TPS54560 buck then steps the 12 V down to a 5 V rail for the driver display and the USB-C and USB-A ports people use to charge their phones. I picked the TPS54560 because it easily handles the several amps the display and charging ports can pull together, and its wide input range holds up well on a noisy 12 V vehicle bus. On the input side there's fusing and transient protection, so a fault on the bus doesn't make it further into the system.
