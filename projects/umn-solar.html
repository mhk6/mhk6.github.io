UMN Solar Vehicle, Power-Distribution Board

Altium · TPS54560 · power

A power board I designed for the University of Minnesota Solar Vehicle Project. It's fabbed, soldered, and running on this year's car.

What it needed to do

The solar car's driver interface runs on a Raspberry Pi. The team moved it to a Pi HAT that needed 12 V and more current than the usual 5 V setup, so I designed a separate board just to handle that power cleanly and keep it apart from the rest of the electronics. A large connector links it to the battery and to the UI control board, which sends the enable signals.

Built around switching

A high-side P-channel MOSFET works as a load switch, so the UI control board can turn 12 V to the Pi HAT on and off with a logic-level enable signal instead of cutting power somewhere messier. After that, a TPS54560 buck steps the 12 V down to a 5 V rail for the display, the Pi's other needs, and the USB port the driver might plug into. I picked the TPS54560 because it can easily handle the several amps the Pi, screen, and USB can pull together, and its wide input range works well on a noisy 12 V vehicle bus. On the input side there's fusing and transient protection, so a fault on the bus doesn't make it further into the system.

I did the layout, released it, reflow-soldered it, and now it's running on this year's car.
