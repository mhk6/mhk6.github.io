USB-to-UART Programmer

Altium · CP2102N · USB-C

A programmer board I designed during my internship so we could flash an ESP32 system in one step instead of doing it by hand. It plugs into a laptop like a USB stick.

The problem

Before this, the team flashed the ESP32 system with a Raspberry Pi Pico dev board jumpered over to a set of pogo pins. It worked, but it was slow and fiddly to set up, and buying a Pico for every person adds up. So I designed an actual programmer board. The pogo pins land right on it, and the whole thing plugs straight into a laptop.

One-click flashing

My favorite part is the one-click flashing. An ESP32 only goes into its serial bootloader if GPIO0 is held low while it comes out of reset, so by hand you'd hold the boot button, tap reset, and then let go. My board does that by itself with a two-transistor circuit driven by the DTR and RTS lines on the CP2102N bridge. The flashing tool toggles those lines to pull GPIO0 low, pulse reset, and put the chip in its bootloader, and then it resets the chip back to normal once flashing is done. No buttons needed.

What I learned

This was also my first time routing a USB differential pair, and keeping it clean on such a small board was the tricky part. The board ended up becoming the team's standard flashing tool.
