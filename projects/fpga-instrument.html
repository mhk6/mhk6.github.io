FPGA Mixed-Signal Instrument

SystemVerilog · Artix-7 · Vivado · Python

One FPGA that works as three bench instruments at once: a triggered digital oscilloscope, an 8-channel logic analyzer, and a streaming monitor. The data path targets an Artix-7 in SystemVerilog, and a Python app on the PC handles the interface, measurements, and export.

The idea

Good test equipment is expensive, and I think building a simple version of it yourself is one of the best ways to really understand sampling, triggering, and moving data in real time. I also wanted something that would push me into actual digital design on an FPGA, with clock domains, state machines, and on-chip memory, instead of another microcontroller project.

How it works

The analog front end is an external Pmod AD1 read over SPI, and the Artix-7's built-in XADC stays on as a second channel. Everything runs off one 100 MHz clock domain. A capture controller writes samples into a circular BRAM buffer, which is what lets the scope show what happened before the trigger. Once it triggers, the buffer gets frozen and handed off to be streamed out.

The biggest constraint on the whole design is that UART is the bottleneck. The FPGA can capture way faster than UART can send, so the design captures fast into BRAM, sends it over slowly, and aggregates the data for the live stream.

The groundwork

A big part of this project was the groundwork: the architecture, the module hierarchy, a pin plan checked for conflicts, and a bill of materials that comes out to about 30 dollars.
