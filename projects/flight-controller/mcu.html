STM32F405RGT6 · LQFP-64 · 168 MHz · Betaflight

An STM32F405RGT6 running Betaflight at 168 MHz. Almost nothing on this sheet is logic. It's the support circuit the chip needs to run reliably, plus the pin map that connects it to everything else.

Betaflight is the constraint behind most of it. The firmware expects particular peripherals on particular pins, so the pin map wasn't free, and the clock tree isn't either.

01Decoupling

Get this wrong and the board resets at random, with nothing in the logs to explain it.

Every clock edge switches gates that pull a burst of charge in a couple of nanoseconds. If the nearest source of that charge is the regulator 30 mm away, the inductance of the trace between them turns the burst into a voltage sag at the pin, and a big enough sag crosses the core's logic thresholds. A capacitor sitting at the pin shortens that loop and shrinks the sag by one or two orders of magnitude.

So the question is how much capacitance a pin needs. A 100 mA step lasting 2 ns, with 30 mV of droop allowed before the bulk capacitor has to take over:

C≥IΔtΔV=6.7 nF Fitted 100 nF per VDD pin, four of them, so roughly 15× margin on charge.

The margin only collapses if you ask for a very tight droop on a large, slow step, and that case is covered by the 4.7 µF bulk capacitor behind them rather than by making the local ones bigger.

Why bigger capacitors would make it worse

A real ceramic capacitor has series inductance of its own, so it only stays low-impedance up to its self-resonant frequency. Above that it behaves like an inductor and stops decoupling.

fSRF=12πLESLC Bigger packages have more inductance, so they resonate lower and give up the fast edges.

That's why these are 0402 and why there's one per pin instead of one shared part. Each pin gets its own short loop, and the small body keeps the inductance near 0.5 nH. They're X7R rather than Y5V, so the capacitance survives temperature and DC bias.

02Analog supply

VDDA powers the ADC, the internal reference and the PLL. Digital noise reaching the PLL becomes clock jitter, and reaching the ADC it becomes measurement noise, so VDDA gets a ferrite bead and two capacitors instead of a direct connection to the digital rail.

The bead is 120 Ω at 100 MHz. Unlike an inductor it's lossy at high frequency, so it absorbs noise as heat rather than resonating with the capacitors.

The filter corner, and why it's fed from the clean rail
bead in its resistive band, 80 Ω, with 1 µF and 100 nF shunting fc=12πRbC≈1.8 kHz Attenuating from a couple of kHz up, which covers the switching harmonics that would jitter the PLL.

The bead is fed from the clean 3.3 V analog rail rather than the digital one, so VDDA starts from the quietest source on the board. That means the low-noise regulator feeds the gyro and the MCU's analog supply, which costs it another 10 to 20 mA out of 600 mA. VREF+ is tied to VDDA internally on this package, so this network is also the ADC's reference filter.

03Core regulator capacitors

The F405 doesn't run its core from 3.3 V. An internal 1.2 V regulator steps it down, and that regulator's control loop is stabilised by an external capacitor on each of VCAP1 and VCAP2. These aren't decoupling capacitors, they're the compensation network of an on-chip regulator, which makes them the highest-risk passives on the sheet.

Why the ESR matters as much as the value
2.2 µF low-ESR X7R, ESR around 50 mΩ fz=12πESR⋅C≈1.4 MHz ST specifies both a capacitance and an ESR ceiling, because the zero has to land inside a window.

A high-ESR part pushes that zero down toward instability, and an undersized one pushes it out the other side, so ceramic X7R is the safe choice.

LQFP-64 brings two separate taps of the internal regulator out to two pins, and each one needs its own capacitor at the pin. Paralleling both onto one pin and leaving the other bare starves half the regulator, which is a known cause of F405 boot instability.

04Reset and boot

NRST · BOOT0 · PB2

NRST gets a 100 nF capacitor to ground to de-glitch it. BOOT0 has a 10 kΩ pull-down so the chip boots from flash by default, and a momentary button pulls it to 3.3 V to enter the bootloader for DFU recovery over USB. BOOT1 is on PB2 and gets its own 10 kΩ pull-down, since a floating pin there can select the wrong boot mode.

05Clock

8 MHz HSE · PLL to 168 MHz and 48 MHz

The crystal isn't optional. The F405's USB peripheral needs 48 MHz to within 0.25 percent, and this part has no crystal-less recovery mode, so the internal 16 MHz RC oscillator at ±1 percent is nowhere near good enough. The only spec-compliant path is crystal into the PLL.

8 MHz is Betaflight's default for this family and divides cleanly into both results through integer dividers. There's no 32.768 kHz crystal, because Betaflight doesn't need a calendar and no reference target populates one.

Sizing the load capacitors

The two capacitors present the crystal with the load it was specified for. Wrong values and it either runs off frequency, which breaks USB, or doesn't start. The crystal sees them effectively in series, plus the stray capacitance of the traces and pins.

crystal rated 20 pF, stray around 10 pF Cext=2(CL−CS)=20 pF On each leg, C0G dielectric. X7R would drift with temperature and pull the oscillator off frequency.

There's also a 0 Ω resistor in the OSC_OUT leg. It's a place to add series resistance later if a crystal turns out to be overdriven, without respinning the board. For an 8 MHz fundamental the loop gain margin is large, so it stays at 0 Ω, and it's always populated because leaving it empty would open the oscillator path.
